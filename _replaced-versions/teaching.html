Teaching & Educational Philosophy

Translating abstract physical models into intuitive, tactile, and multimodal learning experiences rooted in cognitive theory and active experimentation.

MANIFESTO

Core Principles

学びの本質と探究の精神

Six foundational tenets guiding instructional practice, inquiry design, and classroom engagement.

I believe genuine understanding in the physical sciences goes far beyond memorizing formulas and definitions. Effective education empowers students to ask questions, design experiments, analyze empirical evidence, and link abstract classroom concepts to real-world phenomena.

01

Learning by Doing

Active Experimentation

Fostering intuitive grasp through physical demonstrations, hands-on lab experiments, and tactile applications before introducing formal mathematical abstractions.

02

Curiosity & Questioning

Inquiry-Based Learning

Cultivating an open inquiry environment where questioning established assumptions, evaluating multiple models, and embracing cognitive doubt is celebrated as the engine of discovery.

03

Scientific Thinking

Empirical Reasoning

Developing structured habits of systematic observation, hypothesis testing, error analysis, and evidence-based argument rather than unquestioned rote compliance.

04

Inclusive Education

Universal Design (UDL)

Creating multimodal learning pathways that accommodate diverse cognitive abilities, varying learning paces, and educational backgrounds without diluting conceptual depth.

05

Technology-Enhanced

Cognitive Multimedia

Leveraging digital animations, interactive simulations, and computational thinking to visually unpack non-intuitive physics phenomena like electromagnetic wave polarization.

06

Real-World Connections

Contextualized Physics

Connecting abstract mathematical models directly to everyday human experiences—from why leaves appear green under optical absorption to the mechanics of smartphone camera sensors.
